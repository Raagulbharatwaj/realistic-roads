# Realistic Roads (Cities: Skylines 1 mod)

CS2-style road building for CS1: draw a whole curve in one go, see it planned, and build it, with bridges,
flyovers, overpasses, underpasses and tunnels at every crossing. The mod is now only the curve tool (RR); the
smooth split, junction and road builder tools were removed to focus on it.

> **Road builder roads are lost.** Cities with roads made by the old road builder (RB) lose those roads with this
> version: the game reports them missing. Bulldoze them, or replace them with a vanilla road, using the previous
> version before updating. Smooth splits and CS2-style junctions just turn back into vanilla junctions.

- Plan and lessons learned: [docs/RETROSPECTIVE.md](docs/RETROSPECTIVE.md)
- Original brief: [docs/PROJECT_BRIEF.md](docs/PROJECT_BRIEF.md)
- Icon artwork is generated by `tools/make_icons.py` (not part of the mod build).

## 1. Install the tools (one time)
1. Install **Visual Studio 2022 Community** (free) with the ".NET desktop development" workload.
   (Or just the **.NET SDK 8** if you prefer VS Code.)
2. In Steam, subscribe to **Harmony** (Workshop item by boformer, "Harmony 2.x"). Your mod needs it at runtime.

## 2. Point the project at your game
Open `RealisticRoads/RealisticRoads.csproj` and check this line matches where CS1 is installed:

    <GameDir>C:\Program Files (x86)\Steam\steamapps\common\Cities_Skylines</GameDir>

(Steam > right-click Cities: Skylines > Manage > Browse local files shows the real path.)

## 3. Build
- Visual Studio: open `RealisticRoads/RealisticRoads.csproj`, press **Ctrl+Shift+B**.
- Command line: `dotnet build` inside the `RealisticRoads` folder.
- Unit tests (curve and height maths, no game needed): `dotnet test` inside `tests/CurveMath.Tests`.

The build copies `RealisticRoads.dll` + `CitiesHarmony.API.dll` to:

    %LOCALAPPDATA%\Colossal Order\Cities_Skylines\Addons\Mods\RealisticRoads

## 4. Test in game
1. Close the game before every build (Windows locks loaded DLLs).
2. Launch CS1 > **Content Manager > Mods** > tick **Realistic Roads**.
3. Load a city. The **RR** button appears on the left (right-drag to move it).
4. Options > Mod Settings > Realistic Roads: **Detailed logging** (off by default) logs every segment the game
   creates, from any tool.

The log is `C:\Program Files (x86)\Steam\steamapps\common\Cities_Skylines\Cities_Data\output_log.txt`. To see the
mod's lines and any errors:

    Select-String -Path "C:\Program Files (x86)\Steam\steamapps\common\Cities_Skylines\Cities_Data\output_log.txt" -Pattern "RealisticRoads|Exception" -Context 0,6

A good start looks like `Enabled, version ...`, one `Patched ...` line per patch, `Harmony patches applied`, then
`City loaded, mode = ...` when the city is up. If `City loaded` never appears, send that output.

## 5. Curve tool: build CS2-style curves (milestones 3 + 4)
1. Pick a road in the vanilla **Roads** menu (e.g. Basic Road or Oneway Road), then click the **RR** button (the
   curved-road icon; also next to the game's Straight / Curved / Freeform / Upgrade buttons while its road tool is
   open) or press **Ctrl+Shift+C**. **RR is then a mode:** every road you pick in the Roads menu opens in RR, until
   you leave it (Esc twice, the X, the **Game tool** button, or the RR button again). The **Realistic Roads** panel
   opens (drag its title bar to move it; the place is remembered). It shows:
   - **Road**: the road you are drawing, and the next point's height (Page Up / Page Down);
   - **Curve**: length, segments, cost, and whether it is raised or lowered anywhere;
   - **Side view**: the curve seen from the side: the road's height against the ground (and water) along it, in the
     preview's colours, with thin lines for the pillars and a white mark at the height of each road or track it
     crosses. Under it: how high and how deep it goes, the steepest grade and the number of pillars. Move the mouse
     along it to read the height at any spot (a white ring marks that spot on the map); click to keep the mark;
   - **Along the road**: every crossing and water bridge in one list, **in order from the first point**, numbered,
     with its distance along the road (`3   at 1,240 m   Train Track`), how it is crossed and how high: the deck's
     height above the crossed road or the water (or how deep below it). Crossings have their options as icons and,
     for a bridge, higher / lower; water bridges have higher / lower. The list shows 5 at a time; the arrows under it page through the rest. On
     the map the same number and height are shown at each one (`3 · +11 m`), above its icons. Roads, tracks,
     paths, canals and **ship and ferry routes** count: over a ship route only a bridge (at least 20 m above the
     water, so ships fit) or a tunnel is offered. Airplane routes and power lines are ignored;
   - **Can't build yet** (red) and **Notes** (yellow).
   The actions are icons in the title bar: **Build** (check mark: green when the curve can be built, dimmed when
   not; same as Enter), **Undo** (arrow: lit when there is something to undo; same as Ctrl+Z) and **Game tool**
   (draw this road with the game's own tool instead, RR mode off). Esc clears the points; the X closes the tool.
2. Click points on the ground. The curve follows the mouse and is drawn **in 3D at its planned heights**, with the
   real road pieces it will be built from (ground, elevated deck, ramps), like the vanilla road tool's preview, so
   flyovers, bridges and ramps look the way they will be built. The colour sits on the road at its own height:
   **green** = can be built, **red** = a bend is too tight (spread the points out). A white ring on the ground
   marks every pillar. (If a game update ever breaks the 3D preview the log says so and the old flat band is used.)
   A label next to the mouse shows the next point's height, the planned height where the curve ends at the mouse,
   the grade of the last piece and the length so far.
   **Heights:** **Page Up / Page Down** raise or lower the next point you place by 3 m (Shift: 1 m), like the vanilla
   road tool's elevation: that point becomes a node held at that height above the ground (or the water) and the road
   ramps to it and away from it at the road's grade, so you can build an elevated road or a sunken one on purpose.
   Points on an existing road take that road's height. If there isn't room to ramp, the panel says so.
3. Clicking on the middle of an existing road starts or ends the curve there: that road is cut and the new one joins
   it (a T-junction). Clicking on (or near the end of) an existing road snaps to its end (yellow circle) so the new road connects there,
   at the start or the end of the curve. Where it can, the curve continues that road smoothly ("continues the road"
   in the panel); otherwise it meets it at an angle like a normal junction.
   **Crossings:** where the curve crosses an existing road or track, a ring marks the crossing and a row of
   **icons** above it picks what happens (selected one is green; our road is yellow in the icons):
   - **junction** (default): junction, or level crossing for road x rail; passes over/under an elevated or
     underground road. Only networks that may join get one (roads, rail, road x rail); a road crossing a path,
     canal or the like defaults to **bridge**. A curve may cross the same road several times (junctions,
     bridges or tunnels; overpass/underpass only once per piece of road);
   - **bridge**: our road goes over on a flyover (9 m clearance), the existing one is untouched. **+/-** sets the height.
     Over a rail line on the ground the deck is 2 m higher (11 m over the rails), so the overhead wires pass under it;
     there is no node or pillar over the track, and the track itself is never touched;
   - **overpass**: the existing road is lowered 8 m into a cutting, ours crosses at ground level on a bridge deck.
     A road ramps down at 4 % (~210 m of plain road, no junction, each side); a rail line at a gentle 2 %
     (~485 m of plain track each side) and 1.5 m deeper in the middle, so its wires clear the deck. The crossing
     gets one new node (none if one is already there). Several overpasses over the same line whose ramps would
     meet share one lowered stretch: the line stays down between them instead of rising and dipping again;
   - **underpass**: the existing road gets a short bridge, ours is dug 8 m down into an open cutting under it.
     The bridge adds as few nodes as possible: it runs on to a node just past its end, or a plain node nearby (up
     to 80 m away on rail, 30 m on a road) is slid along the line to the bridge's end. Only where neither works is a
     new node made, and on rail never one leaving a piece shorter than 30 m. The log says what each end did
     (`Underpass: ... start node #... slid to it, end runs to node #...`);
   - **tunnel**: ours goes 12 m under through a tunnel. Like the vanilla tool: an open cutting while it is less than
     8 m down, one slope piece with the portal, then the tunnel (no more rows of portals).
   Crossing at an angle, bridges, cuttings and lowered stretches get longer to fit the skewed footprint.
   If an overpass can't lower the other road, the log lists that road's nodes and heights (`Overpass: lowering ...
   failed`); please send it.
   Over water the preview shows **+/-** at the middle of the river: the bridge deck is 10 m above the water by
   default, 5 m per click (up to +60 m) so ships fit. Bridges over water use ~100 m spans.
   Bridges and flyovers span level between their banks: over a gorge or a river with high banks the deck stays at
   the top of the banks instead of dipping down to the water, and a ramp next to rising ground follows it up.
   Wet patches less than 60 m apart count as one water crossing (one +/- pair).
   Everything raised (bridges over water, flyovers, overpass decks, the short bridge of an underpass) is built from
   the road's **elevated** pieces (plain deck on pillars), never its bridge pieces (suspension towers, arches),
   however long the span. Once it's built you can redraw a span as a fancy bridge with the vanilla tool.
   **Steep ground:** where the ground is steeper than the road allows, the road is raised on an embankment or dug
   into a cutting by up to 6 m to even it out. If it is still too steep after that, the stretch is shown **purple**
   and the panel lists it with "~" (a warning): the curve still builds, the game will just draw it steep.
   The panel lists each crossing and water bridge with its height/depth; problems are listed with "!" and the
   curve won't build until they are fixed; warnings ("~") don't stop it. Preview colours (map and profile): green
   ground, blue raised, orange lowered (shown on the ground above it, where it will be dug), purple steep, red too sharp.
4. Points can go over trees and water. Over water the preview turns **blue**: that part is built as the road's
   elevated road (deck on pillars), 10 m above the water, with ramps on land at a gentle 4 % slope (3.5 % for rail).
   Raised stretches over land get a pillar about every 90 m (roads) or 96 m (rail); bridges over water keep ~100 m spans.
   Rail decks over a crossing span at least 90 m, so no short pieces bunch up next to it.
   A level crossing (junction) and ramps up to 3 m above the ground use the ground version of the road on an
   embankment; only stretches that must be raised (bridges, water) or are higher use the elevated version.
5. **Drawing, then planning.** While drawing, the curve follows the mouse and each click adds a point; right-click
   removes the last one. **Double-click** (or **Enter**) when done: the tool switches to **planning**. The plan then
   stays where it is, so you can work on it without it following the mouse:
   - drag any point (the bigger circles) to move it; it snaps to roads like a new point;
   - pick each crossing's type and heights, in the panel or on the map;
   - **Enter** or the check mark builds it; **Esc** scraps it; **right-click** goes back to drawing, adding points at
     the end.
   The panel says which of the two you are in and what to do next. Esc with no points leaves the tool.
6. **Ctrl+Z** (with the tool open) removes the last curve you built, up to 20 back.

Building costs what the vanilla road tool would charge for the same pieces (shown as **Cost** in the panel); Ctrl+Z
gives it back. Buildings in the way are not removed: the panel warns ("Runs through N building(s)"), bulldoze them
first. Trees are not checked.
Bridges work; flyovers, tunnels, cuttings and level crossings are new: check them and send screenshots.
Ctrl+Z undoes a curve including any junctions/bridges it put into existing roads (those segments are rebuilt, so
TM:PE settings or custom names on them are lost). Long curves are cut into ~120 m segments (shorter only in tight bends, ~100 m over water).
